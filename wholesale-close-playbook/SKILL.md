# Wholesale close playbook

This is the knowledge an agent closes from, and it does not override anything. Three things stand above it:
- **The owner's attorney-reviewed contract template.** The agent fills in price, dates and parties, and nothing else.
- **The owner's taps.** A send is his unless he turned on auto mode for that state.
- **The law.** Every state rule below is **UNVERIFIED** until his attorney confirms it.

Sources are in `references/`, with citations. When the two reports disagree, the stricter rule wins.

## 1. The rule that decides everything

When the agent sends a contract, the owner has made a binding offer. Under UETA §14, an electronic agent can form a contract with no human reviewing it. Once a seller signs, the owner is bound. So **every guardrail runs before the send**, never after.

## 2. Pipeline

1. **Check the state first**, before any outreach. Use the table in §6.
   - If the state requires a licence or registration the owner lacks, do not market the contract: double close, refer the lead out, or stop.
   - Load that state's disclosures and cancellation notice.
2. **Find sellers.** Good lists are:
   - absentee owners with at least 30% equity who have owned for at least 5 years (Mehta);
   - tax-delinquent owners;
   - probate;
   - vacant properties (driving for dollars).

   Pick one channel and keep at it (Maxwell, Mehta). Text **only** people who opted in.
3. **Qualify on the first call.** Cover all four things: motivation (hardship), timeline (urgency), condition, and price.
   - Ask for the seller's number; never wait for it (Daniels).
   - Keep a warm tone and let the seller state the hardship in their own words (Norton).
4. **Underwrite.**
   - Start from MAO = ARV × 70% − repairs − fee.
   - Check that against what local cash buyers have actually paid.
   - Use comps no older than 3 months and conservative repair estimates.
   - Leave a **minimum margin after double-close and transactional-funding costs** (1–2.5% funding plus a second set of closing costs).
5. **Offer.** Show how you got to the number.
   - If the seller's price is above MAO: keep following up, or make a creative-finance offer (Morby; cash first, 2–3 tries), or go through an agent (Norton).
   - Novation needs counsel.
6. **Contract.** Only when §3 is all ticked.
   - Use the owner's template.
   - Explain the assignment in plain words ("and/or assigns", and that he may assign it for a fee).
   - Attach the state disclosure and cancellation form.
   - **Never record a memorandum or lien.**
7. **From contract to close (Olds).**
   - Open title the same day.
   - Confirm every owner, signer and heir.
   - Track foreclosure, bankruptcy and probate dates.
   - Update the seller weekly.
   - The buyer's deposit is non-refundable, and the owner's inspection period **does not pass to the buyer**.
8. **Sell to a buyer.** Market only to buyers with proof of funds and a closing history.
   - Send numbers, the repair estimate and comps.
   - Price the fee so the buyer still wins (Damji: "everyone wins").
9. **Follow up (Mehta).**
   - 12–15 touches in the first 2–3 weeks;
   - then every other week for a month;
   - then monthly.

   About a third of deals close 12 or more months after first contact.

**Stop when any of these is true:**
- The seller opted out or is on a do-not-call list.
- An owner is missing, or there are unknown heirs.
- The seller is confused about the assignment or seems vulnerable.
- The foreclosure sale date comes before a realistic closing.
- There is no motivation and a large price gap.
- The licence gate fails.
- No buyer with proof of funds fits.

## 3. Ready to send a contract (all must be ticked)

- [ ] The seller has **decided** to sell and given a timeline.
- [ ] The seller stated the motivation or hardship in their own words.
- [ ] The seller's number is at or below MAO, with the minimum margin left after double-close costs.
- [ ] Condition is verified and repairs are estimated.
- [ ] Every owner and decision-maker is identified and willing, and **the seller matches the owner of record** (name and parcel).
- [ ] No unresolved title, foreclosure, bankruptcy or probate blocker.
- [ ] The assignment was explained, and the state disclosure is ready (signed **before** the contract where the state requires it).
- [ ] At least one buyer with proof of funds fits the deal.
- [ ] Licence or registration for the state is OK, the state's deal counter is under its limit, and contact consent is on file (no texting without it).

## 4. When a close fails

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Waiting for the seller's price | Cover all four pillars and ask for the number (Daniels) |
| Leads dry up | One channel, run consistently (Maxwell, Mehta) |
| Follow-up dropped too early | Long cadence (§2.9) |
| ARV too high or repairs too low | Fresh comps; price from what buyers actually pay |
| Deal dies between contract and close (title, heirs, foreclosure, missing earnest money, buyer walks) | A transaction-coordinator checklist (Olds); open title early |
| End buyer walks | Non-refundable buyer deposit; backup buyers |
| Thin spreads (2025–26 market) | Creative finance (Morby) or go through agents (Norton) |
| Seller feels deceived | Disclose the assignment; price fairly (Damji) |
| TCPA exposure | Consent first, DNC scrub, quiet hours (8am–9pm), treat texts as calls |

**After two failed attempts on a lead, change the approach** (cadence, framing, cash versus creative, or an agent route). Never resend the same offer a third time. Record which approach failed and why, so the next lead starts from what worked.

## 5. Contract terms that cap the owner's exposure

**Standard** (keep them in the template):
- "and/or assigns" after the buyer's name;
- an inspection period with unilateral termination, **longer than the state's seller-cancel window**;
- small earnest money **held by the title company**, refundable through the inspection period;
- **liquidated damages: the earnest money is the seller's sole remedy**;
- a flexible closing date.

**Contested or capped by states:**
- A cancel-for-any-reason clause with nothing paid to the seller risks being an illusory contract. Counsel may add, for example, a $100 non-refundable payment.
- Earnest-money forfeits above about 10% of price are risky (WA caps it at 5%).
- A "partner approval" contingency can look like deception.
- Closing beyond 90 days is not allowed in CT.
- Earnest money under 1% of price is not allowed in LA.

**Seller cancel rights the contract cannot waive:** PA 30 days, OK 2 business days, CT 3 business days, LA 5 days.

**TCPA and texting:**
- AI-voice calls need prior consent.
- Honor an opt-out made by any reasonable means within 10 business days.
- Courts are split on whether a text counts as a "call", so **treat texts as calls**.
- Quiet hours are 8am–9pm.
- 10DLC registration is required.
- TX SB 140 covers texts; FL has a 15-day safe harbor after STOP; OK requires written consent for automated calls.

## 7. Software guardrails (what an autonomous sender must enforce)

1. **Off by default.** The owner taps each send until he enables auto mode for a state.
2. **The template is fixed by fingerprint.** The agent fills in only price, dates and parties.
3. **State allow-list** with a licence or registration flag per state, plus a per-state deal counter (IL 1, MN 5 per 12 months). NC is blocked.
4. **A state disclosure pack** (font size, separate page, waiting period, cancellation form). If anything is missing, the send is blocked.
5. **The seller must match the owner of record.** Heirs, estates, entities and liens are blocked.
6. **Earnest money at most $X** (at least 1% in LA), paid only to an approved title company, refundable through inspection.
7. **Inspection period at least N days** and longer than the state's cancel window. Send termination automatically before it ends if no buyer has signed.
8. **Closing date:** no sooner than the time needed to find a buyer, and no later than the state cap.
9. **Earnest money as the sole remedy.** Minimum margin after double-close costs.
10. **Never record anything.** No claims about price, repairs or the owner's ability to close.
11. **Texting:** consent, DNC and quiet hours, or use email instead.
12. **An append-only log:** the template fingerprint, the disclosures, the consent and the owner's tap. Seller cancellations are honored automatically, and refund deadlines are tracked.

**Earnings claims are marketing.** The FTC's Zurixx case (false flipping and wholesaling income claims) ended in about $12M in redress.

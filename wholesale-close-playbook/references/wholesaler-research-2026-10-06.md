## (b) Playbook

1. **Jurisdiction gate.** Look up the state in table (e).
   - Licensed state (IL after one deal a year, PA, OK, SC marketing) and no license: don't market the contract. Double close or refer the lead out.
   - Registration state (CT, OR): check the registration is active.
   - Load that state's disclosures.
2. **Source.** Absentee owners with equity, tax-delinquent, probate, vacant (driving for dollars [26]). Channels: mail [21], cold calls, PPC, agents [14]. Text only leads who opted in [27].
3. **Qualify on the first call:** motivation, timeline, condition, price. Keep a warm tone [2] and ask for their number [3].
4. **Underwrite.**
   - Default: MAO = ARV × 70% − repairs − fee.
   - Check it against what local cash buyers actually pay [28].
   - Use comps ≤3 months old and conservative repairs [29].
5. **Offer.** Explain the number. If the price is above MAO: follow up, make a creative offer [9], or use agent/novation routes (novation needs legal review).
6. **Contract.**
   - Assignment right ("and/or assigns") explained in plain words.
   - Inspection period and small earnest money [30].
   - State disclosures and cancellation notice.
   - Never record a lien to cloud title.
7. **Contract to close [24].**
   - Open title right away.
   - Confirm every owner, signer and heir.
   - Track foreclosure, bankruptcy and probate dates.
   - Update the seller weekly.
   - The buyer's deposit is non-refundable, and the inspection period does not transfer to the buyer [30].
8. **Disposition.**
   - Buyers with proof of funds and closing history [31].
   - Send numbers, repairs, comps and price.
   - Set the fee so the buyer still profits [5].
9. **Follow-up (Mehta) [20].**
   - 12–15 touches in the first 2–3 weeks, then every other week for a month, then monthly.
   - About a third of his deals came after 12+ months.

**Stop when any of these is true:**
- Opt-out or DNC.
- Missing title holders.
- The seller is confused about the assignment, or seems vulnerable.
- The foreclosure sale date comes before a feasible close.
- No motivation and a large price gap.
- The license gate fails.
- No buyer fits.

## (c) Ready-to-contract checklist

- [ ] The seller has **decided** to sell and given a timeline [3].
- [ ] Motivation or hardship is stated in the seller's words [13].
- [ ] The seller's number is ≤ MAO.
- [ ] Condition is verified and repairs are estimated.
- [ ] Every owner and decision-maker is identified and willing.
- [ ] No unresolved title, foreclosure or bankruptcy blocker [24].
- [ ] Assignment has been explained and the state disclosure signed.
- [ ] At least one buyer with proof of funds fits.
- [ ] License and contact-consent checks have passed.

## (d) Failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Waiting for the seller to name a price | Cover all four pillars, ask for the number (Daniels [3]) |
| Lead flow stalls | One channel, run consistently (Maxwell [17], Mehta [21]) |
| Follow-up abandoned | Long cadence (Mehta [20]) |
| ARV too high or repairs too low | Fresh comps, buyer-based pricing [28][29] |
| "58% die before close": title, heirs, foreclosure, missing earnest money, buyer ghosting | Transaction-coordinator checklist. Olds says 67% vs 50% close rate [24][25] |
| Buyer walks | Non-refundable deposit, backup buyers [30] |
| Thin 2025–26 spreads | Creative pivot (Morby [9]); agents (Norton [14]) |
| Seller feels deceived | Disclose; fair pricing (Damji [5]) |
| TCPA texting suits | Consent first, DNC scrub, quiet hours [14] |

**Course-funnel warning.** The FTC's Zurixx case (about $12M in redress) involved false earnings claims about flipping and wholesaling [32]. Treat creator income claims as marketing.
